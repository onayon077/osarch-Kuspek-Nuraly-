# Ticket 2 — Four Computer Builds

**Prepared:** 2026-11-24  
**Vendor:** [Белый Ветер / Shop.kz](https://shop.kz/) (Kazakhstan)  
**Currency:** Kazakhstan tenge (KZT)  
**Budget limit:** 3,000,000 KZT, including one monitor and licensed OS per PC.

I did not choose four identical computers. Each employee has different daily tasks, so I spent more money on the parts that matter for their work. The component prices below are supported by 19 unique Shop.kz browser screenshots taken on **2026-11-24** (see `evidence/vendor-quotes/`). The same screenshot is reused when a part appears in several builds. The earlier AOC Q27G4XF monitor became unavailable, so I replaced it for the developer and designer with the AOC Q27B3CF2/01 at 114,990 KZT each. Stock and shipping may change before purchase. PCPartPicker lists must still be created and reviewed for compatibility.

## Build 1 — Accountant
**Load:** Excel, 1C/accounting software, PDF documents, email, and 5-10 browser tabs for about 8 hours daily.
**Bottleneck:** 16 GB of RAM may become the first limit with very large spreadsheets or several accounting applications open. The 1 TB SSD keeps file access fast.
**Trade-off:** I chose 16 GB RAM and integrated Radeon Vega graphics instead of 32 GB and a dedicated GPU. It is enough for standard accounting and saves money.
**Alternative not chosen:** A Ryzen 7 or dedicated GPU would cost more but would bring little value to accounting tasks.

**Trade-off:** I spent more on 32 GB RAM, a webcam, and a headset but kept the Ryzen 5 and integrated GPU rather than buying a Ryzen 7 or graphics card.
**Alternative not chosen:** 16 GB RAM is cheaper but may cause slowdowns during customer calls with many tabs open.

## Build 3 — Web Developer
**Load:** VS Code, Docker/WSL2, a local database, 2-4 containers, test services, and 10-20 browser tabs together for 8 hours daily.
**Bottleneck:** CPU and RAM are most important. Running containers and local databases together can use many cores and much of the 32 GB RAM.
**Trade-off:** I chose an 8-core/16-thread Ryzen 7 5700G, 32 GB RAM, and a QHD screen instead of a dedicated GPU. Programming does not usually need a powerful graphics card.
**Alternative not chosen:** An AM5/DDR5 platform would have a better long-term CPU upgrade path but costs more. AM4 is the budget trade-off here.

## Build 4 — Designer
**Load:** Photoshop, Illustrator, Figma, many images and layers, and occasional short video or GPU-accelerated effects for about 8 hours daily.
**Bottleneck:** A graphics card, sufficient RAM, and the quality of the monitor matter for graphics work. Very large image projects could also use more RAM.
**Trade-off:** I spent more on the RTX 5060, 32 GB RAM, and a 27-inch QHD IPS monitor, but chose Ryzen 5 5600 instead of Ryzen 7. I prioritized GPU and display over CPU.
**Alternative not chosen:** A professionally calibrated, wider-gamut monitor or a GPU with more VRAM would be better for advanced color-critical work or large 3D scenes, but would cost more.

**Budget decision:** The plan is under the maximum. The remaining amount is a reserve for price changes, delivery, extra cables, installation or business needs. It is not included as a purchased component in this estimate.

**Important limits:** These figures price the listed components and Windows licences, not Microsoft Office, Adobe subscriptions, backup services, professional monitor calibration, assembly labor, or printer/network infrastructure. The director should confirm which of these are required. For business management and security, Windows 11 Pro may be preferable; this estimate uses legal Windows 11 Home licences to keep the cost clear. One licence is budgeted for each of the four PCs.

**Future upgrades:** Sales Manager RAM is the first likely upgrade (replace the 2x16 GB kit if required; the A520 board has only two slots). The developer can add RAM in the unused slots on the B550 board. The designer may eventually need a GPU with more than 8 GB VRAM or a more accurate monitor. The AM4 platform limits later CPU upgrades compared with AM5.
